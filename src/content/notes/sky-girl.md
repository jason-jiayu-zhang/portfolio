Any entrance besides the main doors is futile. They caught you outside last time. The only way you’d get inside and do anything about the plans set in motion tonight is a disguise. You’ve already been spotted with the dress you were planning to wear: the moon-lit gown you’d left in the woods. All because of someone just a bit too curious. Hopefully they’ll warn the queen. Either way it should be fine. The man didn’t see your face. The pursuers didn’t see your face.

This time a simpler approach. A black gown to match the black hair of birth. Black on black on black. Everything, everything black. With the woods only a couple minutes away, there’s no chance they’ll catch me if they chase. All I need to do is convince the queen to leave the room three minutes after the moon rises. They will act on five.

You already missed your first chance. You cannot be stopped now. Not when lives are at stake.

𐑺 天 𐑺

Security here is a joke.

Pickpocket someone in line with a similar face and the guards at the gate won’t bat an eye. All that means is the assassins will have an even easier time getting into this event. You would think after a royal assassination the guards would be more alert, but instead they seem relaxed. Maybe they weren’t fans of the king. His opulence I suppose was a bit excessive, but nothing he did was inherently immoral. Either way, he’s dead now. And the queen will be next if I don’t hurry.

The moat greets you once past the outermost wall. Across it stretches a massive drawbridge, likely 100 feet across. Yet this time the bridge seems to stretch across miles. Time slows as every step seems more exaggerated and more lengthy. Slowly, as fear grasps your soul, you grind to a halt, unable to move.

“Are you alright?”

The question jumps you out of your stupor. Snapping your head around, you meet eyes with a young boy, their face far too familiar.

“It’s you. The omnipotent boy. You know your actions got the king killed right?”

The boy, confused and clearly surprised, whimpers, “What? I-I didn’t do anything. I wasn’t even there that night…”

But his intonation is wrong. His mannerisms are wrong. He’s fidgeting far more than when you last saw him. It’s like he’s a different soul entirely.

“Oh… nevermind,” you stammer. “I must have had the wrong person. Sorry about that.” Hastily, you scurry towards the doors, the shock having scared your previous stupor away. All you can think of is utmost haste.

Seconds will kill.

𐑺 天 𐑺

The throne room is so crowded, even more so than during the king’s declaration. People wait anxiously for the arrival of the queen. Finally, as the sun dips beneath the horizon, as the moon rises into the night sky, the lights shut off, leaving only the hue of night sky, the hue of your dress, present as the queen emerges from the darkness.

Instantly, someone breaks away from the slow pace of the crowd, dashing forward towards the throne. Everyone rushes to get out of their way, sending waves of shoves through the packs. Caught by one of these, you almost stumble to the ground, left to be crushed under the heels of anyone and everyone.

Instantly, you turn to intercept them. They’re a bit further from the throne than you, maybe 7 steps in the direction of your 10. They’ll be at least a few seconds away from the firing range by the time you catch them. The mob, unaware of the two figures barreling towards them, panics and begin storming the exit. The people flow around both of you, trying to find the path of least resistance to freedom.

10 seconds to intercept. Your tunnel vision kicks in, seeing only your target and the path to them.

5 seconds to intercept. Just a few feet away, if not for the people blocking your path.

3 seconds to intercept. They’re within arms reach. Stretching out you grab the hem of their cloak.

2 seconds to intercept. You pull the cloak down, causing the figure to stumble towards you.

1 second to inte-

A pair of arms wraps themselves around you, hugging you, restraining you.

“Let go of me you imbecile,” you growl.

A brief pause before a response comes.

“I don’t think I will,” the boy snickers. “No I don’t think I will.”

That voice. Tilting your head back you take a look at the boy in front of you. Dread fills your soul as the glaring blue eyes pierce your disguise.

“No, no, no-”

The boy smiles, managing only a single phrase: “Did you miss me?”

Before the hall glows with luminescent rays.

Before the hall echoes with blood curdling screams.

Before the hall darkens with omnipotent darkness.

Darkness of the night sky.
