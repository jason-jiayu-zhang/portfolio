The king’s speech, although lengthy and exhausting, spreads itself across everyone in the great hall. From the humans in front to the beastfolk underneath you, the entire audience reels involuntarily, as the weight of words crowns their minds. You see everyone’s posture rise slightly, even a few of them stumbling forward, as the king begins the end of his rehearsal. With previous practice, you’ve built a resistance to his verbal commune, but you can still feel drawn ever so slightly to the king’s ideals.

⚞ 日 ⚟

Nothing escapes your vision from your spot, a perch on a wooden beam above the center of the room. Today, everyone is dressed in costume. Mythical beasts, red-feathered soldiers, and other fantastical characters spread across the floor. Yet, lurking within the festivity, you see a few shady figures with earplugs set in their ears. A pouch of coin handed discreetly to a guard. An assortment of bottles and bags shared between a couple of hooded figures. A needle plunged into the neck of an old man in the front of the crowd. It seems even a party cannot quell the actions of the black market. However, what catches your eye is a glimpse of gold from outside the room. Through one of the windows, you catch two picturesque blue eyes and the hem of a golden dress ducking beneath the window.

You’re forced to take a long route toward the window as some of the beams in the ceiling creak under pressure. As you head closer to investigate, you catch more glimpses of gold. Snaking in a loop, you nimbly navigate the mess of supports before finally reaching a vantage point over the window. Just as you arrive, the girl peeks her head up again and scans the room to make sure no one is watching her. Across her shoulders is a golden dress, one that is very uncooperative with her attempt to stay inconspicuous. Almost glowing around her, the dress gives off an aura of light, dressed like the sun. After seeing no attention towards her, the sun girl raises the window, and slips in, only to be met by your hand over her mouth, holding her in place.

Her face is cold, chilled by the winter night. She shivers slightly, both from cold and from fear. Her eyes flick to you, panicked, at the notion of being captured. She struggles to break free, but you’ve practiced exactly this scenario. Motioning your head towards a side door, you lead her towards a room off the main hall: a small storage room. Locking the door, you take your hand off her mouth, allowing her to speak.

With a slight shiver in her voice, she stammers, “P-please, you need to let me go, I need to warn the king-”

Raising a finger to your lips, you motion her to silence, hand her a scrap of paper and a piece of charcoal, and motion for her to write.

Quickly, she scribbles down a line.

> I need to warn the king.

Reaching for the paper, you respond in kind.

> Usually, people with good will toward the crown enter the grand hall through the front doors. Most others are not so friendly. Tell me what you need.
> How do I know I can trust you?
> You can’t. But if you tell me nothing, I have all the reason to send you to the guards.
> You’re not that type.
> Correct, sun girl. Usually, you’d be dead already. But the early death of children is something I’d prefer to avoid.
> You have to let the king know that a trap has been set in the hall. I’m not sure what exactly will happen, but, if nothing happens, he will die tonight.
> Aren’t you a bit too young for a job like this?
> That’s beside the point! You need to let me warn someon

Before she can finish, you snatch the paper from her hands and lean towards her ear, whispering, “I know everything already. What happens is not in the place of mortals to disrupt the lines of fate.”

Just then, all the lights of the grand hall extinguish themselves, as the bright flash of a beam of fire flies through the center of the king’s chest.
